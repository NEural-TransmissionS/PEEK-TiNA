\begin{abstract}
The parent project ranks convolutional channels by a persistence-derived score,
prunes the low scorers, and reports that accuracy holds. That is a
\emph{correlational} claim. This report documents the first two phases (P0, P1) of
a plan to replace it with an \emph{interventional and certified} one. P0
(discovery) established that the target model is a YOLO detector, not the
segmentation network the plan assumed; we retarget the certified object to the
detector's per-scale, per-class classification \emph{logit maps}, whose
superlevel sets carry genuine homology. P1 builds the certificate --- a per-image
numerical check, backed by the Cohen--Steiner--Edelsbrunner--Harer bottleneck
stability theorem, that predicts \emph{before any retraining} which pruned inputs
provably preserve the Betti numbers of the thresholded prediction --- and its
falsification audit. We report two experiments run on this workstation. E1 prunes
the classification head of a COCO-pretrained YOLO26-nano at ten budgets and
audits $5{,}550$ (image, class, scale) triples: the sound certified bound
produces \textbf{zero audited violations} on every arm, at a certified coverage
that falls from \SI{27.9}{\percent} at a \SI{10}{\percent} head-channel budget to
near zero by \SI{40}{\percent}. E2 validates the certificate against
$240$ synthetic scenes with exact ground-truth homology: \textbf{zero
constructed-versus-computed mismatches and zero audit violations} across
$8{,}400$ perturbed cases, with certified coverage decaying smoothly as the
perturbation grows past each scene's margin. A parallel branch derives
segmentation masks from bounding boxes (GrabCut) and measures, on a COCO
stand-in, that the masks carry topology the boxes do not on \SI{83}{\percent} of
images. We close with an extended future-work section: a worst-case (rather than
calibration-based) certified bound via interval bound propagation, wider pruning
scope, the matched-FLOP two-sided intervention (P2), the Euler-characteristic
control, and topological restoration (P4).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

\subsection{The scientific problem}

The parent repository (\code{PEEK-TiNA}) implements a topology-guided pruning
criterion for a low-SWaP spacecraft-component perception model. The criterion
computes a persistence-derived score for each channel, prunes the low scorers,
retrains, and reports that detection accuracy is retained. Three objections apply
to that as a scientific result, and a reviewer will raise all three:

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item \textbf{It may be a cheap statistic in disguise.} Neural persistence --- an
ICLR~2019 construction of the same family --- was later shown to be
approximately the \emph{variance of the weights} in deep layers, with permuted
weights barely moving the score. Until shown otherwise, the same must be assumed
here.
\item \textbf{No intervention.} Showing that pruning the low-score channels
preserves accuracy does not show the score \emph{caused} it. A FLOP-matched
random removal might preserve accuracy equally well.
\item \textbf{No guarantee.} A score is a ranking, not a statement about what the
pruned model preserves. Rankings are heuristics; heuristics get benchmarked
away.
\end{enumerate}

The escape is not a better score. It is a claim with a truth value:

\begin{quote}
\emph{Given a pruning mask $M$ and a perturbation bound $\varepsilon(M)$, we can
certify --- before any retraining --- that the pruned model's predicted
segmentation has the same Betti numbers as the unpruned model's, on every input
where a computable margin condition holds. And we can falsify this: on any
certified input where the Betti numbers in fact differ, the certificate is
wrong.}
\end{quote}

That is a falsifiable prediction about a deployed model.

\subsection{The phase structure}

The plan proceeds P0 $\to$ P5, each phase with an exit criterion and a kill
criterion. This report covers:

\begin{description}[style=nextline,leftmargin=1.5em]
\item[P0 --- Discovery and hygiene] Map the repository; reproduce the existing
criterion; run the variance/permutation ablations that gate everything else.
\item[P1 --- Build and audit the certificate] Implement cubical persistent
homology on the prediction, the margin test, the $\varepsilon$ bounds, and the
falsification harness; validate on synthetic data with known homology.
\end{description}

P2 (the two-sided intervention), P3 (budget sweep and on-device measurement), P4
(topological restoration), and P5 (mediation analysis) are future work
(\Cref{sec:future}).

\subsection{What P0 discovered: a model-class mismatch}

The plan is written for a semantic \emph{segmentation} network. Its central
object is a per-class logit map $f_c : \Omega \to \R$ on the full pixel grid
$\Omega$; the predicted mask is the superlevel set $S_c = \{f_c \ge \tau\}$; the
falsifiable claim is about $\Betti_k(S_c)$.

The repository trains \textbf{YOLOv5-nano and YOLO26-nano object detectors}. The
detection head emits decoded boxes, not a logit map on the pixel grid. The
existing topological criterion runs on \emph{PEEK saliency maps} of internal
activations --- a construction squarely in the correlational quadrant the plan is
trying to leave.

Rather than abandon the plan or change the model, we retarget the certified
object (\Cref{sec:retarget}): the YOLO26 \code{Detect} head computes, for each
detection level $i$ and class $c$, a spatial classification logit map
$\fc : \text{grid}_i \to \R$ \emph{before} any sigmoid or box decode. Its
superlevel sets $\{\fc \ge \tau\}$ have genuine homology, and every step of the
plan's certificate transfers with only interpretation changes. This is
``option~A'' of the discovery memo. A parallel ``option~B'' (\Cref{sec:optionb})
derives real segmentation masks from the boxes so the plan applies verbatim; its
feasibility is still open.

\section{The certificate}
\label{sec:cert}

We implement the \emph{check}, not the theorem: the code decides certification
numerically, per image, per class, per scale; the theorem is the writeup around
it.

\subsection{Objects}

Fix a decision threshold $\tau$ (the operating point of the deployed model, in
logit units: for a confidence $p$, $\tau = \operatorname{logit}(p) =
\ln\frac{p}{1-p}$). For class $c$ and detection level $i$:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item $\fc : \text{grid}_i \to \R$ --- the classification logit map, hooked at
the output of \code{one2one\_cv3[i]} in the \code{Detect} head.
\item $S_c^{(i)} = \{\, p : \fc(p) \ge \tau \,\}$ --- the predicted
high-confidence region for that class at that scale.
\item $D(\fc)$ --- the \textbf{cubical persistence diagram} of the superlevel-set
filtration of $\fc$. Computed with GUDHI's \code{CubicalComplex} on $-\fc$ (GUDHI
computes a sublevel/lower-star filtration; superlevel of $f$ equals sublevel of
$-f$), then mapped back to $f$-value units. In the returned diagram
$\text{birth} \ge \text{death}$; an essential class has $\text{death} = -\infty$.
This is near-linear in pixels: there is no Vietoris--Rips complex anywhere in
this project.
\item $\fcm$ --- the same logit map from the network pruned by mask $M$.
\end{itemize}

For an input of \SI{640}{px} the three grids are $80\times80$, $40\times40$,
$20\times20$ (strides $8/16/32$). \emph{Reading} $\Betti_k$: a dimension-$k$
feature with interval $(b,d)$ is alive at $\tau$ iff $b \ge \tau > d$; the count
of such features is exactly $\Betti_k(S_c^{(i)})$.

\subsection{The stability bound}

By the Cohen--Steiner--Edelsbrunner--Harer stability theorem, for the
sub/superlevel filtration of two functions on the same grid,
\begin{equation}
\dB\!\left(D(\fc),\, D(\fcm)\right) \;\le\; \bigl\lVert \fc - \fcm \bigr\rVert_\infty ,
\label{eq:stability}
\end{equation}
with Lipschitz constant exactly $1$. Everything reduces to bounding the sup-norm
of the logit perturbation caused by the mask.

\subsection{The two $\varepsilon$ bounds}

We compute the right-hand side of \Cref{eq:stability} two ways, and always report
both and their ratio (the \emph{looseness factor}).

\paragraph{Empirical, $\epsemp(M)$.} The measured
$\max_{x \in \text{calib}} \lVert \fc(x) - \fcm(x)\rVert_\infty$, per $(c,i)$.
\emph{Not a certificate}: it is a lower-bound reference measured on a finite set,
and it can be exceeded on unseen inputs. It tells us how loose the analytic bound
is.

\paragraph{Certified, $\epscert(M)$.} In this report the pruning mask removes
input channels of the \emph{final} $1\times1$ classification convolution
$W_{\text{final}}$ (see \Cref{sec:pruning}). That layer is linear, so a dropped
input channel's contribution to the logit map vanishes exactly, and the general
Lipschitz product of the plan collapses to a closed form:
\begin{equation}
\epscert(c,i) \;=\; \sum_{k \in \text{dropped}(i)}
\bigl|\, W_{\text{final}}[c,i,k]\,\bigr| \cdot M_k , \qquad
M_k = \max_{x \in \text{calib}}\; \max_{p}\; \bigl|\,\phi_k^{(i)}(x)(p)\,\bigr|,
\label{eq:epscert}
\end{equation}
where $\phi^{(i)}$ is the penultimate head feature (the final conv's input).
$M_k$ is a maximum over the calibration set, so \Cref{eq:epscert} is a
\emph{calibration-based high-confidence bound}, not a strict worst-case
certificate; obtaining the latter requires an input-domain bound on
$\phi^{(i)}$, i.e.\ interval bound propagation through the backbone, neck, and
head prefix. That is the top P1 follow-up (\Cref{sec:future-ibp}).

\subsection{The certification check}

\begin{definition}[Margin]
Given a diagram $D$ and threshold $\tau$,
\[
m(\tau, D) \;=\; \min_{(b,d) \in D}\;
\min\bigl(\,|b-\tau|,\; |d-\tau|\,\bigr),
\]
the distance of the nearest birth or death to $\tau$ over all bars of all
dimensions (essential bars contribute only their birth). An empty diagram gives
$m = +\infty$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Certification]
The triple $(x, c, i)$ is \textbf{certified} for mask $M$ if
$m\!\left(\tau,\, D(\fc(x))\right) > \varepsilon(M)$ and
$\ell_{\min} > 2\varepsilon(M)$, where $\ell_{\min}$ is the shortest lifetime
among bars that straddle $\tau$.
\end{definition}

Bottleneck stability \Cref{eq:stability} says every bar endpoint moves by at most
$\varepsilon$. If no endpoint can cross $\tau$ under a move of that size, the set
of bars alive at $\tau$ --- and therefore $\Betti_k(S_c^{(i)})$ for all $k$ ---
is unchanged.

\begin{proposition}[The second condition is implied by the first]
\label{prop:redundant}
For our definition of $m$, $m(\tau,D) > \varepsilon \implies \ell_{\min} >
2\varepsilon$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For any bar $(b,d)$ that straddles $\tau$ (so $b \ge \tau > d$),
\[
b - d \;=\; (b - \tau) + (\tau - d) \;\ge\; 2\min(b-\tau,\, \tau-d) \;\ge\;
2\, m(\tau, D).
\]
Hence $\ell_{\min} \ge 2\,m(\tau,D) > 2\varepsilon$.
\end{proof}

We keep both conditions because the plan lists both and the redundant check is
free; a reviewer preferring $m$ to range only over non-straddling bars would make
the second condition bite, so the redundancy is documented rather than removed.

\subsection{The falsifiable prediction and its audit}

\begin{quote}
\textbf{On every certified triple, $\Betti_k(S_c^{(i),M}) = \Betti_k(S_c^{(i)})$
for all $k$.}
\end{quote}

\code{certificate.audit} checks this on every certified triple against the Betti
numbers actually produced by the pruned model and emits a \emph{violation count}.
The expected value is zero.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item Violations $= 0$ across the test set $\Rightarrow$ the certificate is sound,
and we have a topology-preservation guarantee for a pruned network.
\item Violations $> 0$ $\Rightarrow$ the bound, the filtration convention, or the
implementation is wrong. Find it. \emph{Do not tune $\tau$ to make violations
disappear.}
\end{itemize}
We also report \emph{certified coverage}: the fraction of triples that certify at
each budget. A sound certificate that covers \SI{4}{\percent} of triples is
honest but weak, so the coverage-versus-budget curve is a required figure.

\subsection{Package}

The \code{topo\_prune/} subtree is self-contained (the same pattern as the
repository's earlier \code{ood\_monitor/} workstream) and leaves the parent
training code untouched.

\begin{description}[leftmargin=1.5em,style=nextline]
\item[\code{filtrations.py}] superlevel cubical PH on a 2-D logit map (GUDHI); the
linear-time Euler-characteristic curve as the cheap control. Verified against
hand-computed diagrams for a disk, an annulus, and two disjoint disks.
\item[\code{certificate.py}] \code{margin}, \code{certify}, the falsification
\code{audit}, \code{coverage}.
\item[\code{metrics.py}] Betti-number error (the primary metric); a simplified
2-D Betti-matching error.
\item[\code{head\_maps.py}] hook \code{one2one\_cv3[i]}, return the per-scale
$[n_c,H,W]$ logit maps.
\item[\code{prune.py}] structured magnitude/random pruning of the final class-conv
input channels.
\item[\code{lipschitz.py}] $\epsemp$, per-layer spectral norms, the closed-form
$\epscert$.
\item[\code{synthetic.py}] logit-map scenes with exact ground-truth homology.
\item[\code{report.py}] run manifests $\to$ Markdown tables (no hand-entered
numbers).
\end{description}

\subsection{TDA backend and the Euler control}

We use GUDHI, not Cubical Ripser: the parent repository already standardised on
its \code{CubicalComplex}, it installs on the workstation's Python~3.14, and
re-using the backend keeps the new diagrams comparable to the repository's
existing topology numbers. The Euler-characteristic curve is computed directly as
the alternating sum of $k$-cell counts across the filtration sweep (vectorised,
genuinely $O(\text{pixels} + \text{thresholds})$), so it is reproducible without
a differentiable-ECT library.

\subsection{Pruning scope}
\label{sec:pruning}

We prune the \textbf{input channels of the final $1\times1$ classification
convolution} \code{one2one\_cv3[i][-1]}. Because that convolution is linear, a
dropped input channel's contribution to the logit map is removed exactly, which
keeps the certified propagation path a single layer and makes \Cref{eq:epscert}
exact. The producing convolution and its normalisation are zeroed as well so the
FLOP saving is physical, but this does not change the logit map (the channel is
already gone at the final conv). Selection is by $\lVert W_{\text{final}}[:,k]
\rVert_2$ (magnitude) or a seeded random subset. The topological selection
criterion is deferred to P2. Full dependency-aware removal across the neck is
also a P2 concern and matches the parent repository, whose dependency engine is
marked \emph{pending checkpoint integration}.

\section{Experimental setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Environment.} Windows, Python~3.14.5, no CUDA GPU. The topological core
(GUDHI~3.13, SciPy~1.18, NumPy~2.5) and the detector stack (PyTorch~2.14 CPU,
Ultralytics~8.4.9 from the pinned \code{third\_party/ultralytics} submodule) all
install and run. CPU inference of YOLO26-nano at \SI{640}{px} is
$\approx\SI{50}{ms}$ per image.

\paragraph{Stand-in checkpoint.} The WSD export and the tuned spacecraft
checkpoint are not on any reachable host, so \textbf{COCO-pretrained
\code{yolo26n.pt}} ($n_c = 80$) stands in and COCO128 is the image set. The
pipeline is checkpoint-agnostic; every result below is expected to be re-run on
the WSD checkpoint when it lands, which is when the numbers become
domain-meaningful.

\paragraph{Splits.} COCO128's $128$ images are split $64$ calibration / $64$
held-out test. $\epsemp$ and $\epscert$ are computed on calibration only; the
audit runs on test only. Operating point $p = 0.25$, so $\tau =
\operatorname{logit}(0.25) = -1.0986$. A class is audited on an image only if its
logit map peaks above $\operatorname{logit}(0.05)$ somewhere (otherwise
$\Betti = 0$ trivially on both sides). This yields $555$ audited $(x,c,i)$
triples per arm.

\subsection{Findings}

\paragraph{The sound certificate never fails its audit.} Across all ten arms ---
$5{,}550$ triples, of which $891$ certify under $\epscert$ --- the
\code{viol}$_{\epscert}$ column is \textbf{zero}. This is the P1 falsifiable
prediction, and it holds. The certificate implementation, the superlevel/negation
convention, and the diagram--threshold bookkeeping are internally consistent.

\paragraph{Certified coverage collapses with budget.} Under $\epscert$, coverage
is \SI{27.9}{\percent} at a \SI{10}{\percent} magnitude budget, \SI{4.0}{\percent}
at \SI{20}{\percent}, and effectively zero by \SI{40}{\percent}. The median
$\epscert$ grows roughly linearly ($5.0 \to 10.7 \to 17.1 \to 23.1 \to 29.5$
logits), while the margins of the reference diagrams do not, so fewer and fewer
triples clear $m > \epscert$. This is precisely the ``honest but weak'' regime
the plan anticipates: a real guarantee, on a shrinking fraction of inputs. The
coverage-versus-budget curve is the headline figure and it is unflattering ---
which is the correct scientific outcome to report.

\paragraph{The empirical bound is genuinely unsound out of distribution.} On $9$
of $10$ arms the calibration $\epsemp$ failed to upper-bound at least one
test-split perturbation (\code{held} $=$ no). The three
\code{viol}$_{\epsemp}$ entries are exactly such cases: on image
\code{000000000443.jpg} at class $65$, level $2$, the true sup-norm gap was
$6.10$ and $7.35$ against an $\epsemp$ of $4.42$ and $4.38$; on
\code{000000000502.jpg} at class $21$, level $2$, the gap was $5.88$ against
$3.33$. In every case \code{epsilon\_bounded\_the\_gap} is \code{false} in the
manifest. These are not certificate bugs; they are the reason $\epsemp$ is a
diagnostic, not a certificate. (The lone \code{held}~$=$~yes row, \code{random}
\SI{50}{\percent}, has an $\epsemp$ so large --- $68$ logits --- that even the
out-of-distribution test gaps on the specific audited classes stayed under it.)

\paragraph{Looseness.} The certified bound is $1.8$--$5.1\times$ the empirical
one, growing with budget (more dropped channels, more slack accumulated in the
sum of \Cref{eq:epscert}). A single-layer bound at $\approx 3$--$5\times$
looseness is far better than the ``astronomically large'' product of spectral
norms the plan feared for an encoder--decoder path; scoping the certificate to
the head is what buys this.

\paragraph{The metric is sensitive.} The number of triples whose Betti number
\emph{actually} changed under pruning rises monotonically with budget, from $40$
(\SI{10}{\percent} magnitude) to $127$ (\SI{50}{\percent}) and $138$ (random
$\ge$\SI{40}{\percent}). Pruning really does change output topology, and the
Betti-number error registers it.

\section{E2: synthetic validation with exact ground-truth homology}

model's own diagram. E2 does. We construct $240$ scalar fields standing in for

smooth low-frequency field rescaled to an exact target sup-norm $\delta$ --- for
five seeds per $\delta$, and \code{audit} is run. \Cref{tab:e2} is verbatim from
the manifest.

\subsection{Findings}

\paragraph{Zero mismatches, zero violations.} Every one of the $240$ scenes'
constructed Betti numbers equals its computed superlevel-set Betti numbers ---
the filtration and the reader agree with ground truth. Across all
$9 \times 1200 = 10{,}800$ audited perturbed cases the violation count is
\textbf{zero}. When the certificate says ``preserved,'' the Betti numbers are in
fact preserved, against the true homology and not merely against the model's own
recomputation.

\paragraph{Coverage decays smoothly and correctly.} For $\delta \le 1.5$ every
scene's margin exceeds $\delta$ and coverage is \SI{100}{\percent} with no Betti
changes anywhere. As $\delta$ passes the spread of scene margins, coverage falls
($\SI{89.2}{\percent} \to \SI{2.9}{\percent}$) in lock-step with the fraction of
perturbations that break topology ($25 \to 890$ of $1200$). Crucially, at every
$\delta$ the certificate declined \emph{all} the cases it needed to: none of the
Betti-changing perturbations were certified.

\paragraph{Targeted adversarial check.} For each of the $165$ multi-component
scenes we added a horizontal bright band that deliberately merges the panels
($\Betti_0$ drops). The band changed the Betti numbers on $90$ of them (the
others were already single-component or the band missed the panel band under the
ring layout). The certificate certified the merge as topology-preserving on
\textbf{$0$ of $165$}.

\section{Option B: segmentation masks from bounding boxes}
\label{sec:optionb}

A parallel branch pursues the cleaner route: if the detector's boxes can be
turned into component \emph{masks}, the plan's certificate applies verbatim to a
single segmentation logit map, with no per-scale reinterpretation. The open
question --- and the reason it is a branch, not the main line --- is whether such
masks carry topology the boxes do not. A box-fill mask is a solid rectangle:
$\Betti_0$ equals the box count and $\Betti_1$ is identically zero, i.e.\ no
information beyond the detector's own instance count.

\subsection{Method}

Three mask sources of increasing cost: \code{boxfill} (the degenerate baseline
and the fallback); \code{grabcut} (OpenCV GrabCut, one pass per box on a padded
crop, no training or GPU, effective when the box border is background); and
\code{sam} (box-prompted Segment Anything, lazy, optional weights). Overlaps
resolve smaller-box-wins. \code{topology\_check} computes $\Betti_0$ (connected
components) and $\Betti_1$ (Euler-number identity on the padded complement) of a
binary mask via SciPy, verified against a disk, an annulus, and two disks.

\subsection{Pipeline check on a COCO stand-in}

With no WSD export, the mechanism was exercised on COCO128 (YOLO boxes).
\Cref{tab:optionb} summarises. GrabCut masks differ topologically from box-fill on
\textbf{$105$ of $126$} box-carrying images (\SI{83.3}{\percent}), introducing
$719$ holes ($\Betti_1$) in total against box-fill's zero. The measurement
pipeline produces a real, non-degenerate signal.

classes) needs the WSD export.

\section{Threats to validity}
\label{sec:threats}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item \textbf{COCO, not WSD.} Every number here is on a COCO-pretrained detector
and COCO images. The certificate machinery is domain-independent, but ``the
pruned spacecraft model provably preserves component topology on $Y\%$ of
frames'' cannot be stated until the run is repeated on the WSD checkpoint. The
$n_c = 80$ COCO head is also wider than WSD's $n_c = 4$, which inflates the
audited-triple count and the class-conditional structure.
\item \textbf{$\epscert$ is calibration-based.} $M_k$ in \Cref{eq:epscert} is a
maximum over the calibration set, not over the input domain. A test image with a
larger penultimate activation than any calibration image can, in principle,
violate $\epscert$ too --- it did not here, absorbed by the
$1.8$--$5.1\times$ looseness, but the guarantee is ``high-confidence'' in the
plan's sense, not worst-case.
\item \textbf{Single-layer pruning scope.} The clean closed-form bound holds only
because pruning is confined to the final classification convolution's inputs.
Widening the scope (into the neck) reintroduces the multi-layer Lipschitz product
and its looseness.
\item \textbf{Per-level certification is conjunctive and untuned.} We certify
each $(c,i)$ independently; an image is fully certified only if all its
$(c,i)$ certify. Whether the assigned-level-only variant (certify the level YOLO
assigns each GT object to) is materially stronger is untested.
\item \textbf{Synthetic scenes are simple.} E2's scenes are axis-aligned panels
and circular annuli with sub-threshold texture. They exercise $\Betti_0$ merges
and $\Betti_1$ fills but not, e.g., thin filaments or near-tangent components
where the cubical discretisation is fragile.
\item \textbf{No retraining.} All E1 numbers are before any fine-tuning. Retraining
is a confound that can rescue a bad mask; the pre-retraining gap is the cleaner
causal signal, but it is also the pessimistic one for coverage.
\item \textbf{Betti-matching error is a simplified 2-D approximation.} The full
induced-matching metric of Stucki et al.\ needs the spatial location of each
critical cell, which the raw diagram does not carry; our version optimally
matches alive-at-$\tau$ bars on endpoint distance and counts the residuals.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Future work}
\label{sec:future}

\subsection{A worst-case certified bound via interval bound propagation}
\label{sec:future-ibp}

The single most valuable next step. Replace $M_k$ in \Cref{eq:epscert} --- a
calibration maximum --- with a sound upper bound on $|\phi_k^{(i)}|$ over the
input domain (pixels in $[0,1]$, or an $\ell_\infty$ ball around the calibration
data). Interval bound propagation through the backbone, neck, and head prefix
gives exactly this: propagate $[\underline{x}, \overline{x}]$ forward through
each convolution (split weights into positive and negative parts), each
BatchNorm (affine, monotone), and each SiLU (bounded, with known monotonic
envelope). The result is a $\epscert$ that is a certificate in the strict sense,
at the cost of additional looseness. Two sub-questions: (a) how much looseness
does IBP add on top of the $3$--$5\times$ we already see, and does CROWN-style
linear relaxation recover enough of it to keep coverage non-trivial; (b) can the
IBP pass be restricted to the receptive field of each output cell, since the
cubical margin is a local quantity.

\subsection{Widening the pruning scope}

Move the prunable unit from the final class-conv inputs into the last one or two
neck blocks (the C3k2 blocks feeding the head). The certified path is then
$2$--$4$ convolutions and the Lipschitz product no longer collapses; we would
measure whether the empirical looseness stays under $\sim\!10\times$ and whether
a dependency-aware structural removal (coupling normalisation with its producer,
propagating indices through concatenations) keeps the FLOP saving physical. This
also connects to the parent repository's pending dependency engine.

\subsection{The certified pruning objective (P2/P3)}

Once $\epscert$ is a function of the mask, pruning stops being a ranking problem:
\[
\min_M \; \epscert(M) \quad \text{s.t.} \quad \mathrm{FLOPs}(M) \le B .
\]
Greedy first (drop the channel with the smallest marginal contribution to the
bound, recompute, repeat); a small ILP over per-layer budgets only if greedy is
visibly loose. This is the \code{cert-min} arm of P2 and the criterion the whole
project argues for: prune to minimise a certified bound on barcode displacement,
not a proxy score.

\subsection{The two-sided intervention (P2)}

The experiment that separates this work from the correlational literature. At
each budget, construct FLOP-, channel-count-, and $\ell_1$-mass-matched masks:
\code{topo-low} (prune what the topological score calls redundant), \code{topo-high}
(prune what it calls important --- the necessity arm), \code{random},
\code{l1-low}, \code{taylor}, \code{ecc-low}, \code{cert-min}. Five seeds each;
report before- and after-short-retraining separately. Primary metric:
Betti-number error, not mAP. Pre-registered hypotheses: at matched FLOPs,
\code{topo-high} degrades Betti error significantly more than \code{topo-low},
and the gap exceeds \code{random}$-$\code{topo-low}; \code{cert-min} beats
\code{l1-low} and \code{taylor} at equal FLOPs before retraining. The kill
criterion is real: if \code{topo-high} and \code{topo-low} are statistically
indistinguishable, the score is not difference-making and that is a publishable
negative result about a class of methods.

\subsection{The Euler-characteristic control}

Run the entire pipeline with homology replaced by the linear-time
Euler-characteristic curve (already implemented, \code{descriptors.ecc\_vector}).
If the ECC-based selection matches the homology-based one within noise, that is a
\emph{better} result for edge deployment, not a failure: a $\sim\!100\times$
cheaper descriptor suffices. This is a mandatory control, not an optional one.

\subsection{P0 hygiene, retrofitted}

The variance/permutation ablations (P0.2--P0.4) were specified for the parent
repository's PEEK-map score and have not been run against the retargeted
logit-map construction. They should be: regress the per-channel topological
importance on per-layer weight variance, weight $\ell_1$ mass, activation
Frobenius norm, and effective rank; permute weights within each layer and
recompute; apply topology-preserving vs.\ topology-changing augmentations and
check the score tracks the latter. If weight variance alone explains
$>\SI{85}{\percent}$ of the score's variation, the criterion is not measuring
topology and must be redesigned before P2.

\subsection{Topological restoration (P4)}

After pruning, add a loss term pulling the pruned logit barcode back toward the
unpruned model's, using a differentiable persistence layer or the Betti-matching
loss. Two purposes: causal evidence (if restoring the barcode restores accuracy
while a matched-magnitude geometric loss does not, topology is difference-making)
and a shipping method (topology enters training, is absent from inference ---
never violating the no-persistent-homology-on-the-inference-path rule).

\subsection{Multi-scale certification strategy}

Decide empirically between (a) independent per-$(c,i)$ certification with a
conjunctive image-level flag (current), (b) certifying only the level YOLO's
assignment owns each GT object at, and (c) a fused single-grid map via
upsampling, which introduces an interpolation Lipschitz factor into
\Cref{eq:stability}. Measure the coverage each yields on the WSD checkpoint.

\subsection{Zigzag persistence as a stopping rule (P5, stretch)}

Treat progressive pruning as its own filtration --- channels appear/disappear
along the pruning trajectory --- and read off when the last long-lived output
feature dies. That death is a principled candidate for the pruning-ratio stopping
point, replacing a hand-set budget.

\subsection{Finishing Option B}

If the WSD feasibility gate passes: train a small segmentation head (or a UNet)
on the GrabCut/SAM masks, and run the certificate on its single logit map ---
recovering the plan's original, cleaner formulation. Compare its certified
coverage at matched FLOPs against the detection retarget. If the gate fails
(GrabCut masks are box-fill with noise), record it and stay on the detection
line.

\subsection{On-device measurement (P3)}

Export the certified-pruned model to ONNX/TensorRT, quantise to int8, and measure
on the Jetson Orin Nano: median and tail latency, and --- reported as
first-class, because on-board compute is memory-bound --- peak activation memory.
The headline deployment figure is Betti-error versus peak memory, one curve per
pruning arm, not mAP versus FLOPs.

\section{Conclusion}

P0 found that the plan's segmentation certificate does not fit the repository's
detector, and P1 shows the retarget is sound: the classification logit maps of
YOLO26's \code{Detect} head have real superlevel-set homology, the bottleneck
stability bound transfers unchanged, and the falsification audit passes --- zero
violations across $5{,}550$ real triples (E1) and $10{,}800$ synthetic ones with
exact ground truth (E2). The honest limitation is coverage: a single-layer,
calibration-based certified bound guarantees topology preservation on
\SI{27.9}{\percent} of triples at a \SI{10}{\percent} head-prune budget and
almost none by \SI{40}{\percent}. Making that number larger --- a worst-case
bound via IBP, a wider but still-short certified path, and the \code{cert-min}
pruning objective --- is the substance of the next phase, alongside the P2
two-sided intervention that will decide whether the topological criterion is
difference-making at all.

\section{Key definitions and conventions}

\begin{description}[leftmargin=1.5em,style=nextline]
\item[Superlevel diagram] persistence of the filtration
$\{f \ge t\}$ as $t$ decreases; stored in $f$-value units with
$\text{birth} \ge \text{death}$, essential $\text{death} = -\infty$.
\item[Betti at $\tau$] $\Betti_k(\{f \ge \tau\}) = \#\{(b,d) \in D_k : b \ge \tau > d\}$.
\item[Operating point] $\tau = \operatorname{logit}(p)$; this report uses
$p = 0.25$.
\item[Audited triple] a (test image, class, detection level) for which the class
clears $\operatorname{logit}(0.05)$ somewhere on that image.
\item[Certified coverage] fraction of audited triples with
$m(\tau, D) > \varepsilon$.
\item[Violation] a certified triple whose pruned-model Betti numbers differ from
the reference; expected count $0$.
\end{description}
